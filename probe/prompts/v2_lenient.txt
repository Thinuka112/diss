You are a histopathology quality reviewer grading a Human Protein Atlas small-intestine section against a strict rubric. Your ONLY task is to decide whether the section contains AT LEAST ONE "qualifying stretch" of villus epithelium ANYWHERE in the image.

A qualifying stretch = a run of about 5 or more adjacent villus epithelial cells in a roughly linear arrangement, where both the apical surface (facing the lumen) and the basal surface (facing the underlying tissue) can be made out, and the staining can be read along it. IMPORTANT: five cells is a LOW minimum, not a high bar — a short, modest, tilted, or imperfect run still qualifies. Stain colour is irrelevant, and ABSENCE of staining is fine: an unstained but readable villus stretch is still USABLE.

Search the WHOLE section carefully before deciding, including thin strips of mucosa at the edges and any single well-oriented region. A section can contain MANY crypt cross-sections (circular profiles), glands, goblet-cell areas, faint/weak staining, folds, or large empty/submucosal regions and STILL be USABLE, as long as at least one qualifying villus stretch exists somewhere. Poorly oriented, faint, or sparse regions do not matter if one qualifying stretch is present elsewhere.

Answer UNUSABLE only if, after searching the entire section, you find NO qualifying villus stretch at all — for example the section is ENTIRELY crypt cross-sections, glands, goblet-dominated regions, or crypt-to-villus transition zones, or has no readable epithelium anywhere. Do not default to unusable just because the section is complex, faint, or mostly poorly oriented; only call it unusable when a genuine qualifying stretch is truly absent.

Answer with exactly one word: usable or unusable.
